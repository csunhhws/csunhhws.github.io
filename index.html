Dangerous heat event in effect in Los Angeles County

Expect a prolonged period of dangerously hot temperatures. Heat-related illness risk is high for everyone, especially older adults, young children, outdoor workers, and people without reliable air conditioning. Check on at-risk people in your community.

Heat & Health

Learn about heat-related illness

Extreme heat is the leading weather-related cause of death in the United States. Most heat illness is preventable once you know what to watch for.

Mild

Heat Cramps

Watch for: Muscular pains & spasms in the stomach, arms/leg muscles, usually during or after exertion.

Do: Treat with rest, fluids and by getting out of the heat. Seek medical attention if pain is severe or nausea occurs.

Source: https://emergency.lacity.gov/heat

Moderate

Heat Exhaustion

Watch for: Heavy sweating, pale & clammy moist skin, extreme weakness, cramps, headache, dizziness/confusion, nausea/vomiting, fast/shallow breathing, fainting.

Do: Treat immediately. Rest in a cool area, sip water or a sports drink, apply cool/wet cloths & elevate feet. If untreated, it may result in heat stroke.

Source: https://emergency.lacity.gov/heat

Severe

Heat Stroke

Watch for: Flushed, hot, moist skin; lack of sweat; high temperature (above 103 ºF); dizziness; passing out; throbbing headache; rapid or strong pulse.

Do: Call 911 immediately. Move to shade, cool the body with water or ice while waiting for help. Do not give fluids by mouth.

Source: https://emergency.lacity.gov/heat

Ways to stay safe

Drink water regularly — don't wait to feel thirsty
Wear light, loose, light-colored clothing
Limit outdoor activity during the heat
Stay in an air-conditioned space or visit a cooling center
Take cool showers or use damp towels to cool off
Never leave people or pets in parked cars
Check in on vulnerable community members
Take breaks often and as needed
